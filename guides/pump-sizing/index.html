Pump sizing: centrifugal and positive-displacement, to API, HI and ISO

How to size a centrifugal or positive-displacement pump: system head, NPSH margin, viscosity correction, motor rating, acceleration head and relief valve, with worked examples and a comparison with process simulators.

By Muhammad Anas, process design engineer · updated 2026-11-07 · version 2026.11.12.2

What a pump sizing has to deliver

The process engineer does not choose the pump; the vendor does. The process engineer fixes the duty the vendor must meet and the limits the offer is checked against: rated flow and differential head, NPSH available, maximum suction and discharge pressures, the liquid properties, the motor rating and the minimum flow. Getting these wrong costs either a cavitating pump or an oversized one that runs throttled for 20 years.

Method used in the calculator

H = [Pdst + ρg·zd + ΣΔPdischarge] / ρg − [Psrc + ρg·hs − ΣΔPsuction] / ρg
NPSHa = (Psrc − Pv)/ρg + hs − hf,suction
Pabs = ρ g Q H / η  ·  motor ≥ 1.25 / 1.15 / 1.10 × Pabs (≤ 22, 22–55, > 55 kW, API 610)

Friction is Darcy–Weisbach with the Colebrook–White friction factor and Crane TP-410 K values for every fitting. Strainers and equipment scale with (Q/Qrated)², and the control valve gets its allowance at rated flow. The NPSH margin follows ANSI/HI 9.6.1 by application: for example a ratio of 1.1 or 1 m for petroleum service and 1.3 for boiler feed, whichever is greater. Viscous liquids are converted to an equivalent water duty with the ANSI/HI 9.6.7 factors CQ, CH and Cη, so the vendor can select from water curves.

Worked example: boiler feed water from a deaerator

Duty: water at 150 °C from a deaerator at saturation (3.75 barg), 14 m minimum level above the pump, 180 m³/h normal and 198 m³/h rated, to a steam drum at 115 barg and 30 m elevation, with 3 bar of equipment and a 6 bar control-valve allowance. Six-stage pump at 2980 rpm.

Positive-displacement pumps (API 674, 675, 676)

A plunger pump delivers a pulsing flow, so the liquid in the suction line has to be accelerated on every stroke. The Hydraulic Institute acceleration head is subtracted from the steady NPSH available:

ha = L · v · N · C / (K · g)
C = 0.400 simplex, 0.200 duplex single-acting, 0.115 duplex double-acting, 0.066 triplex, 0.040 quintuplex · K = 1.4 deaerated water … 2.5 compressible liquids

Checked against the published Hydraulic Institute example: a 15 ft suction line at 1.0 ft/s, triplex at 360 rpm, water, gives ha = 7.9 ft. The calculator also draws the instantaneous flow over one revolution (a triplex varies between 90.7 % and 104.7 % of the mean), sizes the plunger, and sizes the relief valve for the full displacement. The driver is not sized on the rated power alone: it must also cover the pump running at the relief condition.

How this compares with a process simulator

A simulator's pump block (Aspen HYSYS, Aspen Plus, UniSim) is a pressure-change unit: you give the outlet pressure and an efficiency, and it returns the head and power. That is the right tool for the heat and material balance, but it is not a pump sizing:

The two work together: take the flow and properties from the simulation, size the pump here, and send the datasheet to the vendor.

Common mistakes

  • Taking NPSH available from a simulator's pump block without the static head.
  • Sizing the motor on the rated power and forgetting the end-of-curve or relief condition.
  • Returning the minimum-flow recycle to the suction line instead of the suction vessel. On a liquid at its bubble point this flashes in the pump.
  • Ignoring the acceleration head on a plunger pump with a long suction line.
